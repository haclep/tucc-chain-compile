## Key Findings

**What the other conversation concluded about the calculation.** On the physics, the campaign banked four exact eigenstates (lowest singlet and lowest triplet at 2.6 and 2.8 bohr) for C₂ in a minimal basis, frozen core, CAS(8e,8o), a 4900-determinant space. Each exact eigenvector was compiled into an ordered product of roughly 2,700–2,850 elementary rotations reproducing the state to 1×10⁻¹³ or better and then rewritten exactly in wave-operator form. The headline results: (1) a singlet–triplet ground-state crossing near 2.77 bohr in this active space — the singlet leads by 17 mHa at 2.6, the triplet pair leads by 2.6 mHa at 2.8; (2) "support pinning" — the exact singlet lives on the identical set of 1108 determinants at 2.348, 2.6, and 2.8 bohr (only the amplitudes move), even at 2.8 where it is no longer the ground state, and the triplet's 1216-determinant set is identical between 2.6 and 2.8, which forced exactly equal chain lengths (2767) and equal growth (1552) at two different geometries; (3) all structural reorganization is deferred to the 2.8→3.0 interval (triplet support turns over — only 624 of 1216 survive — and singlet support migrates down to 660, of which only 364 are inherited and 296 are new), summarized as "energy crosses first; structure reorganizes a fifth of a bohr later."

**The prediction audit — this is the strategically important part.** Two of your own named predictions were falsified and recorded as such. First, the proposed law that chain length per supported determinant is a property of geometry, not state, is dead: at fixed geometry the singlet costs 2.56–2.57 rotations per determinant while the triplet costs 2.28 — a 12% gap — and the apparent agreement at 3.0 bohr was two curves crossing (singlet cost falling 2.89→2.57→2.56→2.30 along the coordinate while the triplet stayed flat near 2.28–2.30), not one shared law. Second, the prediction that the 2.8 singlet support would interpolate between the 1108 and 660 determinant counts was wrong: it is exactly 1108, because pinning killed the interpolation picture entirely. The conversation also banked an operator-level first (at 2.8 the singlet's wave operator fills its entire symmetry-connected block of 1252 monomials, including all 144 determinants where the state carries literally no weight) and left several honest open items (a recurring extremal rotation angle near 1.5506 that appears only at 3.0 bohr; one 2.6-singlet monomial above support with a non-flat tail, probably numerical dust; and an unresolved convention for comparing the degenerate triplet mixtures at 2.6/2.8 against the projected pure component at 3.0).

**The verification result is a genuine asset.** All four compiles were checked by an independent code on a different machine that re-diagonalized from raw integrals, replayed the factor sequences, and matched at machine precision, with deviations tracking the compiler's own error bookkeeping. Independently, the open literature confirms this is not over-engineering: iterative eigensolvers of the Davidson family are known to "root-flip" — as Baiardi et al. (arXiv:1810.08044, JCTC) put it, "in regions with a high density of states, the lowest eigenpair… might not correspond to the target state. This effect, also known as root flipping, lowers the efficiency of the optimization" — exactly the regime C₂ sits in. C₂'s bond order and low-lying state ordering are a genuinely unsettled problem in the electronic-structure literature: Nature Communications (Bull et al., 2019) states that "Bonding in the ground state of C2 is still a matter of controversy, as reasonable arguments may be made for a dicarbon bond order of 2, 3, or 4," and Su (arXiv:1405.1225) notes "the extreme difficulty in calculating the ground state and the low-lying excited states of C2… owing to the multireference character of the wave functions and the near degeneracies which change very rapidly as a function of the C–C distance." A mandatory full dense-diagonalization "census" before trusting any single root is therefore sound physics, not paranoia — and it is the concrete origin story behind the "wrong ground state with all internal checks passing" incident that now justifies the census-first protocol.
